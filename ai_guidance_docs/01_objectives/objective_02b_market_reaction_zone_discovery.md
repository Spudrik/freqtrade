# Objective 02b - Market Reaction And Event-Driven Direction Discovery

## 1. Status And Authority

This objective is now a historical reference, not the active execution queue. The
user superseded its open-ended testing/branching on 26 September 2026 to move to
the comparative paper trial in `objective_03_comparative_paper_trial.md`. Preserve
the findings below, but do not launch remaining batches without a new user request.

It originally superseded the former plan to use
Sieve3 entries and exits as the starting event catalogue. Sieve3 completion, a frozen
Sieve3 surface, and Sieve-derived entry or exit files are not prerequisites for this
objective.

The research starts from the market itself:

1. causal OHLCV history;
2. price levels and zones calculated by existing indicators;
3. clusters of independently produced levels across indicators and timeframes;
4. rational new level-producing indicators when there is a credible reason to test
   them; and
5. selectively available orderbook, news, web, global-market, and other contextual
   sources when their real timestamp-safe coverage permits a bounded question; and
6. tightly scoped `1m` replay around independently selected reaction episodes when
   the evidence-triggered lane in Section 15.1 permits it.

The objective is a governing spine, not an exhaustive ban on new ideas. Agents may
investigate a relevant avenue that was not listed in advance when they can state why it
might improve reaction-zone understanding. Such work must remain bounded, recorded,
and followed by a return to the main plan. An interesting side result must not quietly
replace or indefinitely delay the complete initial scope.

On 3 September 2026 the user explicitly expanded the current direction. Existing
reaction-zone evidence remains the foundation, but the programme now also investigates
the following causal hierarchy:

> slow background conditions -> major scheduled or unexpected event -> Bitcoin,
> Ethereum, or broad-market confirmation -> established-coin or meme-cohort response
> -> coin-local level, cluster, volume, pressure, attention, or liquidity effect ->
> continuation, reversal, or a new settled range.

This authorizes bounded signed-direction and path research inside predeclared event and
post-event episodes. It does not authorize unrestricted prediction at every ordinary
candle, entry/exit construction, profit optimization, position sizing, leverage, or
live/dry-run changes.

## 2. Central Goal

Find price areas that can be calculated using information available at the time and
that are followed by repeatable changes in market behaviour more often, sooner, or
more strongly than suitable control areas.

The first-stage question is:

> Do indicator-derived levels or clusters of levels identify locations where price,
> volume, volatility, participation, or trading pressure changes in a repeatable way?

Isolated levels and level clusters are co-equal research surfaces. A repeatable
reaction around one causally calculated level is a useful finding even when no nearby
cluster is present. A recurring cluster composition is also a useful finding when the
combination repeats across independent episodes and adds information beyond its
contacted component levels, duplicate mechanisms, general level density, and matched
cluster controls. Neither surface must succeed before the other is allowed to remain
a lead.

The reaction-zone stage first establishes whether a calculated location is meaningfully
different from an ordinary location. That work is not abandoned. The three already
frozen fresh-confirmation questions remain fixed and must reach an honest terminal
state without tuning:

1. the recent-OHLCV and broad-market activity forecast;
2. pooled round-number and rolling-distribution convergence for the frozen top-ten
   meme cohort; and
3. daily current-session VWAP-centre reaction.

While later candles accumulate for those confirmations, outcome-blind event/source
reconstruction and coverage work may proceed. Their eventual results must be reviewed
alongside, not allowed to overwrite, the existing level and activity evidence.

The newly authorized stage asks whether major-event information and market confirmation
provide direction, whether that direction transfers to coin groups, whether memes
respond unusually strongly relative to their own normal behaviour, whether local
technical or liquidity conditions modify that response, and whether the resulting move
settles into a new causally measurable range.

The longer programme destination is a set of independently supported, potentially
complementary indications. Each may inform direction, activity, reaction location,
timing, magnitude, or a conditional modification; it need not predict all of them.
On 19 September 2026 the user removed the requirement that abnormal activity and
correct direction must both be high for an individual idea to qualify. Section 15.4
governs separate component assessment and later combination tests. This supersedes
older blanket joint-success gates, but not source integrity, chronological validation,
the breadth-first queue, or the limits on unrestricted direction/trading research.

## 3. What This Objective Is Not

Do not judge a level by:

1. whether an old Sieve strategy entered or exited there;
2. profit after one arbitrarily selected number of candles;
3. whether it catches a remembered crash, breakout, top, or bottom;
4. the best-looking chart examples;
5. a single coin, timeframe, period, or model seed unless the result is explicitly
   retained as a narrow asset-specific exploratory lead;
6. one universal AUC threshold;
7. the number of features a model can consume; or
8. a parameter search whose only explanation is that the resulting score improved.

This stage measures association and incremental information. A calculated level does
not itself prove causation. Even a strong cluster may mark where many market
participants are likely to act without being the cause of their action.

Do not judge an event relationship by:

1. selecting only famous events after seeing their price charts;
2. defining a `major` event from the size of the later market move;
3. treating positive or negative headline tone as the event's economic surprise;
4. treating repeated copies of one story as independent confirmation;
5. treating a larger raw meme-coin percentage move as unusual without comparing it
   with that coin's own normal response and volatility;
6. treating a fixed historical Bitcoin price as a permanent floor; or
7. assuming that waiting longer makes an initially wrong directional call correct.

## 4. Required Plain-Language Concepts

### 4.1 Level

A price coordinate calculated before the tested interaction. Examples include POC,
VAH, VAL, an established pivot, a confirmed trendline value, a rectangle boundary, or
an anchored volume-weighted level.

### 4.2 Zone

A tolerance area around a level. Exact-price contact is usually false precision. Zone
width must use a defensible scale such as ATR, recent range, profile-bin width,
estimated level uncertainty, or observed lower-timeframe execution resolution. The
zone must not be widened after inspecting whether price nearly touched it.

### 4.3 Level cluster

Two or more separately calculated levels close enough in price and time to form one
testable area. A cluster must retain:

1. every component's source family;
2. source timeframe;
3. age and availability time;
4. normalized distance between components;
5. whether components are genuinely independent or share the same underlying pivots,
   window, or formula; and
6. cluster density and width.

Five cosmetic variants of the same calculation do not count as five independent
levels.

### 4.4 Contact episode

The period beginning when price first enters a predeclared zone and ending after it
has left by a predeclared distance or after the episode timeout. Adjacent candles
inside the same zone are one episode, not many independent successes.

### 4.5 Approach side

Whether price arrives from above, below, or was already residing inside the zone. This
is needed to describe movement away from or through a level without turning the study
into an up-versus-down prediction system.

### 4.6 Reaction

A measurable change after contact. It may be:

1. movement away from the level;
2. movement through the level;
3. rapid displacement in either direction;
4. stalling or extended acceptance near the level;
5. repeated crossing;
6. volatility expansion or contraction;
7. volume or participation change; or
8. pressure or candle-behaviour change.

Reaction is not synonymous with reversal and is not automatically useful for trading.

## 5. The Direction Boundary

Signed direction is now authorized only inside the causal event hierarchy and frozen
post-event ranges defined in Section 15.6, plus the evidence-triggered `1m` replay in
Section 15.1. The objective does not authorize a general model that predicts up or down
at every ordinary timestamp.

Before a scheduled event, direction may remain unknowable; the valid question may be
whether unusual activity is likely. After the result becomes available, its surprise
relative to a genuinely reconstructable prior expectation may be used. For an
unexpected event, the programme cannot claim to predict its occurrence; it may test
rapid detection, independent confirmation, the first broad-market response, and later
continuation or reversal.

Allowed questions include:

1. Was movement after contact larger than after matched control contacts?
2. Did either excursion occur sooner?
3. Did activity or volatility change?
4. Did price remain near the zone longer?
5. Did a cluster produce a stronger response than either component alone?
6. Did a predeclared major event precede an unusual BTC, ETH, or broad-market response?
7. Which eligible market leader moved first, using only information available then?
8. Did established coins or memes follow, and was the response unusual for that coin?
9. Did a local level, cluster, volume, pressure, attention, or liquidity state change
   the response beyond the event and market-leader information alone?
10. Did the event move continue, reverse, or settle into a new measurable range?

Still-deferred questions include:

1. Should this become a long entry, short entry, target, or exit?
2. What position size, leverage, stop, or holding rule should be used?
3. Which relationship maximizes profit?
4. Can direction be searched at all ordinary timestamps without an event or qualified
   reaction context?
5. Is a remembered event or chart sufficient evidence for a rule?

Directional outputs remain research measurements, not actions. Report event detection,
reaction, signed direction, continuation/reversal, group transmission, amplification,
coverage, abstention, and uncertainty separately. A model that sees no adequately
confirmed event, leader, or source coverage must be allowed to abstain.

## 6. Research Principles

1. **Causal availability:** every level and feature must be available no later than the
   decision timestamp being tested.
2. **Market behaviour before strategy profit:** measure paths, timing, activity, and
   controls before considering trading actions.
3. **Continuous values before convenient labels:** retain excursion, time, dwell,
   crossing, volume, pressure, and volatility measurements before compressing them
   into reaction classes.
4. **Direct tests before model stories:** first compare observed episodes with matched
   controls; then ask FreqAI whether nonlinear combinations add out-of-sample
   information.
5. **Incremental evidence:** a combined feature block must beat the relevant simpler
   blocks, not just a weak random baseline.
6. **Density fairness:** a method that fills most of the chart with zones must not win
   merely because price is almost always close to one.
7. **Chronological confirmation:** development findings must survive later untouched
   periods.
8. **Market-scope honesty:** broad, coin-group, and asset-specific findings are valid
   only under accurately labelled evidence scopes.
9. **No event memorization:** known crashes, tops, news events, or exceptional moves
   may be diagnostic examples but may not determine the final parameters.
10. **Parsimony:** prefer the simplest causal representation that preserves the
    repeatable effect.
11. **Hierarchical attribution:** establish event, leader, group, and local effects
    separately before combining them.
12. **Whole-event independence:** treat one continuing event as one dependent episode,
    not hundreds of independent candle successes.
13. **Expectation awareness:** separate raw headline tone from surprise relative to
    what was knowable or expected beforehand.
14. **Dynamic leadership:** test BTC, ETH, and broad participation; do not appoint a
    permanent leader from hindsight.
15. **Abstention:** uncertainty, missing sources, unconfirmed events, or conflicting
   market leadership are valid reasons to issue no directional call.

### 6.1 Whole-Episode Conditional Interpretation Guardrail

This objective does not seek universal statements that one indicator, event, or data
source `works`. It seeks conditional probability changes inside a complete market
episode. For every material interpretation, reconstruct as much of this timestamp-safe
state as the proven sources permit:

1. long-running macro, financial, geopolitical, liquidity, and crypto background;
2. the rolling balance of distinct positive, negative, and conflicting narratives;
3. the contemporaneous expectation and information already reflected in price;
4. the new event's novelty, credibility, severity, scope, and surprise;
5. immediate BTC, ETH, or broad-market confirmation or rejection;
6. levels, clusters, liquidity, positioning, and other market structure that may
   modify the response;
7. established-coin, meme-cohort, and coin-local transmission; and
8. the full response path: amplification, suppression, delay, continuation, reversal,
   or settlement.

Assign every input a decision-time role: `background_condition`,
`driver_or_trigger`, `confirmation`, `modifier`, `accumulator`,
`duplicate_or_substitute`, or `outcome`. Roles are not permanent properties of a
column. Pre-event volume may describe background activity or anticipation; immediate
post-event volume may confirm the news response; later volume may be the outcome.

Never condition on a downstream market response and then interpret reduced event
importance as proof that the response, rather than the event, was the root cause. A
later-decision test may use the first response to forecast continuation, but it must be
reported as confirmation or propagation, not as pre-event prediction.

Judge an influence by how it changes the response expected from comparable pre-event
states, not only by the final candle sign. Positive information may reduce an otherwise
likely fall without turning the terminal return positive. An event can cause a strong
initial reaction that is later suppressed, delayed, reversed, shortened, or
overwhelmed by other pressures. Report those paths separately.

A weak standalone average does not disqualify a rational amplifier, suppressor,
reverser, gate, accumulator, override, delay, or transmission blocker. Conversely, do
not save every weak result with a retrospective story. Use development data to expose
possible conditional differences, freeze the condition, and require confirmation on
later whole episodes. Conditions must be measurable before the relevant outcome,
market-rational, adequately supported, and compared with suitable controls.

The central interpretation question is:

> Given the complete information available at this decision time, how did each new
> influence change the balance of competing pressures, under which conditions, over
> what part of the response path, and with what repeatability?

## 7. Data And Source Scope

### 7.1 Primary market data

OHLCV is the primary common surface because it provides the widest consistent history.
Use the available top-ten crypto pairs when suitable data exists. Additional top-ten
pairs may be downloaded through the approved Freqtrade launcher tools when required by
a frozen batch.

The audit must choose multiple source timeframes, observation timeframes, and reaction
horizons based on actual coverage and market meaning. Do not reduce the objective to a
fixed 24-hour target or one fixed candle horizon.

### 7.2 Existing custom indicators

The initial unchanged-indicator atlas should include every adequately supported
causal level family, with distinct families kept separate:

1. Volume Profile POC, VAH, VAL, HVN, LVN, prior-profile levels, value-area state, node
   strength/thinness, profile width, and POC migration;
2. confirmed pivot highs and lows;
3. BOS/CHoCH source levels and retest locations where the underlying level is
   explicitly available;
4. confirmed Trendline V2 support and resistance values, with rank, pivot count, age,
   slope, fit, and stable identity;
5. rectangle, channel, triangle, wedge, compression, and other causal geometry
   boundaries;
6. pattern confirmation, neckline, guide, and target levels when sample size and
   timestamp safety are adequate; and
7. relative-strength or market-state outputs as context where they do not themselves
   define a price coordinate.

Rare pattern levels must not be pooled into common structural levels merely to obtain
more rows.

### 7.3 Generic price and technical references

Simple references are valid baselines or candidate level families when used clearly:

1. previous period highs, lows, opens, closes, and midpoints;
2. rolling extremes and price quantiles;
3. round-number zones;
4. session, weekly, monthly, or event-anchored VWAP where causally constructed;
5. moving averages and volatility bands as comparison levels; and
6. ATR, realized volatility, RSI, MACD, moving-average state, Bollinger state, and
   related standard values as optional context/control features.

Standard technical indicators must not be assumed to contain an edge, and wrapping
them in a new name does not make a new custom indicator.

### 7.4 Rational new indicators

Agents are authorized to select, design, and test additional level-producing
indicators when there is a trader-readable reason to believe they may locate reaction
points better or measure a distinct market mechanism.

A proposed new indicator should normally satisfy these gates before implementation:

1. it produces a price level, bounded price zone, or interpretable level cluster;
2. its market mechanism is distinct from an existing output;
3. it can be calculated causally and preferably vectorized;
4. its expected advantage and likely failure mode can be stated before results;
5. there is enough eligible data to compare it fairly; and
6. it has a small number of meaningful construction choices rather than a large
   arbitrary tuning surface.

Potential avenues include, but are not limited to:

1. lower-timeframe-built higher-timeframe Volume Profiles;
2. adaptive or connected-zone Volume Profile nodes;
3. anchored VWAP families with volume-weighted dispersion zones;
4. swing-price density or repeated-acceptance zones;
5. change-point-anchored profiles or reference levels;
6. volatility-scaled prior-range and session levels;
7. orderbook liquidity walls or depletion zones where genuine historical snapshots
   exist; and
8. independently derived price-volume acceptance or rejection zones.

This list is permission to investigate, not an instruction to implement all of them.

### 7.5 External contextual sources

Orderbook, news, web, global-market, and other available sources are reopened for
bounded use inside this objective. They are optional context, not assumed-ready core
features.

Because coverage is short or inconsistently conditioned:

1. audit real timestamp coverage and freshness before each use;
2. never convert unavailable observations to ordinary zero values;
3. preserve source-availability flags and compare source-present with source-absent
   cases;
4. do not let a short overlap window define a broad historical conclusion;
5. use them first to investigate whether otherwise similar level reactions differ
   during quiet, stressed, news-active, broad-risk, or liquidity-disturbed periods;
6. compare OHLCV-only and OHLCV-plus-source models on the identical eligible rows;
7. treat small-window findings as exploratory leads requiring future accumulation; and
8. do not infer that news or an orderbook state caused a reaction merely because it
   coincided with it.

### 7.6 Historical Event Reconstruction

Build the event catalogue without using later price movement to select or rank events.
Every eligible event record should preserve, where genuinely available:

1. event family and affected entities or markets;
2. scheduled versus unexpected status;
3. the scheduled time or first safe public-availability timestamp;
4. prior expectation and its source, or an explicit `expectation unavailable` state;
5. actual outcome and surprise relative to the prior expectation;
6. first report, independent-source confirmation, official confirmation, and
   implementation timestamps as separate stages;
7. story novelty, persistence, source and entity diversity, and duplicate control;
8. source availability, freshness, gaps, and maximum contributing timestamp; and
9. a slow-background classification calculated only from trailing information.

Event families may include scheduled macroeconomic releases, monetary-policy decisions,
crypto-native failures or regulation, institutional-access changes, trade or tariff
changes, geopolitical escalation or relief, financial-system stress, and structurally
scheduled crypto events. Complete predeclared category surfaces must be used; remembered
examples may check interpretability but cannot be the scored sample.

Use event-type-appropriate time grids. Fast scheduled releases and shocks require
minute-to-day measurements; persistent geopolitical or financial changes may require
day-to-week measurements; structurally anticipated events may require week-to-month
anticipation and aftermath windows. Do not impose one universal `24h` outcome.

### 7.7 Bounded Historical And Live Semantic-Data Pilot

Objective 02b may run one small, outcome-blind data-foundation pilot before further
news/market/level confluence work. This is a bounded exception inside the current
objective; it does not globally resume Objective 02d or authorize a full historical
backfill.

The pilot exists because the live collectors contain substantial raw news, web,
global-market, and orderbook history, while the current news and web records still lack
usable story direction, severity, and confidence. Large row counts alone therefore do
not make those sources analysis-ready.

#### 7.7.1 Freeze And Coverage Audit

Before labeling or testing market outcomes:

1. export immutable snapshots from running SQLite collectors; never run research
   directly against their changing live databases;
2. record exact source, publication, collection, and safe `available_at` coverage,
   including gaps, duplicates, stale periods, and source-specific failures;
3. freeze the eligible source groups, chronological blocks, story-selection rule,
   duplicate rule, and maximum sample size before opening any later price behaviour;
4. preserve unavailable states rather than converting missing observations into
   ordinary zero or quiet-news values;
5. test broken or disabled sources one at a time against temporary output before any
   live configuration change; and
6. fix certificate, parser, cadence, or endpoint root causes rather than disabling
   certificate validation or adding silent fallback behaviour.

Initial isolated source candidates are the two failing ECB feeds, the currently
disabled or stale macro/market-stress/systemic-risk Google Trends groups, and the
stopped Stooq risk groups. Healthy collectors and orderbook streams must not be changed
merely because this pilot exists.

#### 7.7.2 Small Outcome-Blind Story Pilot

Use at most `300` unique story clusters across two chronological blocks. The exact
blocks are selected only after the coverage audit and then frozen. Stratify the sample
across source groups, event families, and simple pre-outcome priority bands so one busy
feed cannot dominate it. Cluster duplicates before semantic labeling.

Neither story selection nor labeling may inspect later market prices, returns, volume,
or the remembered importance of an event. `unclear` is a valid result and must not be
forced into a positive or negative label.

Each pilot story should preserve, where evidence supports it:

1. `story_id`, first and latest safe availability times, source-group count, duplicate
   count, and official-confirmation state;
2. topic, impact channel, affected entities, assets, and markets;
3. apparent direction as `positive`, `negative`, `mixed`, or `unclear`;
4. severity as `minor`, `moderate`, `major`, or `unclear`;
5. novelty, update, escalation, relief, correction, and persistence states;
6. label confidence and short evidence references; and
7. parser, taxonomy, classifier, prompt, model, and source-configuration versions.

An inexpensive model may propose the labels, but deterministic schema validation and
a bounded review sample of up to `40` story clusters are required. An initial `80%`
review-agreement target for direction and severity is an engineering quality gate, not
a claim that the labels predict markets. Disagreement, ambiguity, and abstention rates
must be reported rather than hidden.

#### 7.7.3 Data-Quality Gate

The pilot can reach market testing only when it has:

1. no known `available_at > feature_time` violations;
2. complete source and evidence traceability for every retained label;
3. at least `95%` schema-valid output, with invalid records explicitly quarantined;
4. one story identity for duplicate reports rather than one independent event per
   article;
5. source-present and source-absent states preserved; and
6. no raw data overwritten or silently reinterpreted.

Failure means revise once if the cause is clear, otherwise park the semantic lane. It
does not justify weakening the gates or labeling more history.

#### 7.7.4 Small Incremental Market Test

Freeze all labels before opening market outcomes. On identical eligible BTC and ETH
rows, compare the current simple baseline—article counts, event type, relevance, and
duplicate penalty—with these complete sibling additions:

1. story direction and severity;
2. novelty, escalation, relief, and correction state;
3. independent-source and official-confirmation confluence;
4. one apparently major story; and
5. two or more, then three or more, smaller same-direction stories inside frozen
   trailing windows.

Measure unusual volume, range/volatility, and pressure first; measure signed direction
second and only when the label supports it. Use event-appropriate horizons, beginning
with `1h`, `2h`, `4h`, `8h`, and `24h` for fast events and adding longer windows only
for persistent stories. Hold out whole stories and use chronological blocks so one
continuing event cannot appear in both development and validation.

The semantic block is retained only if it adds repeatable information beyond the
simple baseline in both chronological blocks, survives source/event concentration
checks, and remains intelligible in plain market terms. Direct tests come first;
FreqAI is permitted only when the eligible sample is large enough for whole-story
training and validation. Profit is not an outcome. Apply Section 15.4's role-specific
assessment; useful story information need not predict both activity and direction.
Preserve this pilot's original frozen scores and do not retune its labels to pass.

#### 7.7.5 Stop And Handoff Rules

1. Do not start a full historical backfill, broad FreqAI queue, or production semantic
   collector from this pilot alone.
2. If semantics add value, propose the smallest prospective upgrade and bounded
   backfill blocks before implementation.
3. If semantics do not add value or cannot be labeled reliably, keep the healthy raw
   collectors, park the enrichment path, and use only the already source-ready
   activity/context features.
4. Resume the paused confluence batch only after this decision, using no feature that
   failed its source-readiness gate.

## 8. Causal Construction And Leakage Controls

### 8.1 Level availability

For every level record:

1. the candle on which it was first calculable;
2. its price coordinate and zone width at that time;
3. its source timeframe;
4. its age at contact;
5. whether it moved after becoming available; and
6. whether later data can revise its historical value.

Repainting or retrospectively moved levels are invalid unless the exact historical
version visible at each timestamp can be reconstructed.

### 8.2 Rolling calculations

The contact candle and later reaction candles must not contribute to a profile, pivot,
line, band, or normalization statistic that is presented as pre-contact information.
Where necessary, shift the constructed level by one completed source candle.

### 8.3 Multi-timeframe alignment

A higher-timeframe value becomes available only after its source candle closes. It
must then be carried forward to lower-timeframe observation rows without backfilling
into the unfinished higher-timeframe candle.

No timeframe has automatic precedence. Higher timeframes may create wider or more
persistent zones, while lower timeframes may provide more precise contact and path
measurement. That relationship must be measured.

### 8.4 Overlapping outcomes

Reaction horizons overlap heavily. Chronological train, validation, and test splits
must use suitable purge or embargo gaps so substantially shared future paths do not
appear on both sides of a model boundary.

## 9. Required Reaction Measurements

Measure the full path around each contact episode. Retain raw continuous values before
deriving any summary label.

### 9.1 Price displacement

1. maximum distance away from the zone on the approach side;
2. maximum distance through the zone;
3. maximum absolute displacement in either direction;
4. net displacement, retained for description but not used as the primary directional
   prediction target;
5. away-versus-through path balance;
6. which excursion threshold occurred first; and
7. whether price returned to the zone after leaving.

Normalize movement by ATR, recent realized range, zone width, or another frozen market
scale.

### 9.2 Timing

1. candles and clock time to first material displacement;
2. candles and clock time to away and through thresholds;
3. duration spent inside or near the zone;
4. time between repeated contacts; and
5. time until the level becomes stale or invalid.

### 9.3 Activity and participation

1. change in volume relative to the pre-contact state;
2. change in candle range and realized volatility;
3. body-to-range and close-location changes;
4. directional-volume or CVD-like pressure where causally estimated;
5. wick and rejection behaviour;
6. acceleration or compression; and
7. orderbook activity where valid snapshots exist.

### 9.4 Acceptance and traversal

1. number of crossings;
2. proportion of the observation window spent inside the zone;
3. repeated closes on each side;
4. speed of traversal through a low-liquidity area;
5. whether price stalls near a high-participation area; and
6. whether the zone remains relevant on later contacts.

### 9.5 Horizon grid

Use a predeclared grid of short, medium, and longer observation horizons suitable to
the source and observation timeframes. Report how the response develops across that
grid. Do not choose the best horizon after seeing the result and report only that one.

## 10. Level Attributes And Calibration

The investigation must examine not just the presence of a level but the values the
indicator emits about it.

Applicable attributes include:

1. strength, prominence, thinness, or quality;
2. level age;
3. touch count and time since last touch;
4. distance from current price;
5. zone width and uncertainty;
6. slope or migration speed;
7. source-window length and resolution;
8. level persistence across adjacent calculations;
9. rank and competing-level density; and
10. agreement with independent indicators or timeframes.

Ask whether stronger emitted values correspond monotonically to stronger, faster, or
more reliable reactions. Avoid manufacturing a threshold from one narrow optimum.
Prefer broad stable relationships, plateaus, and sensible ordinal calibration.

## 11. Required Controls

A level is not informative merely because price moves after touching it. Every level
family must face controls appropriate to its construction.

Required control families include:

1. random zones on eligible candles;
2. price-shifted versions of the real level;
3. time-shifted, stale, or expired versions;
4. matched candles with similar volatility, trend, volume, and broad-market state but
   no nearby candidate level;
5. simple prior highs/lows and round numbers;
6. shuffled level identities or quality values;
7. source-family ablations;
8. same-density controls with comparable zone count and total chart coverage; and
9. near-miss episodes that approached but did not contact the declared zone.

Where a result is conditioned on an external source, include the identical eligible
rows without the external feature block and a source-availability control.

## 12. Fair Level-Cluster Research

Level clusters are a central part of this objective, but they create severe selection
and density risks.

### 12.1 Cluster construction

Build clusters using a predeclared normalized price-distance rule. Preserve the exact
components rather than replacing them with an opaque score. Candidate summaries may
include:

1. cluster centre and full width;
2. number of components;
3. number of independent source families;
4. number and ordering of timeframes;
5. weighted and unweighted level dispersion;
6. oldest and newest component age;
7. strongest component attributes; and
8. whether levels sit above, below, or around current price before contact.

### 12.2 Incremental cluster evidence

A cluster is useful only if it improves on:

1. each component family alone;
2. the strongest single component;
3. a matched random cluster with the same component count and width;
4. a same-family duplicate cluster; and
5. a general high-level-density control.

Use leave-one-component-family-out ablations to identify whether the cluster is truly
synergistic or merely another name for one dominant level.

Treat a repeated cluster pattern as the recurrence of a causally defined component
relationship, not the recurrence of one remembered price or historical event. Preserve
the exact components actually contacted by price. Select any anchor or strongest
component using information available before the reaction, then compare
multi-component contact episodes with matched episodes where that same anchor
component was contacted alone. Do not present the identical future candles from one
episode as independent cluster and component outcomes.

Single-level evidence remains valid alongside cluster research. Report isolated-level,
cluster-member, and cluster-pattern results separately so a useful isolated level is
not discarded merely because it lacks confluence, and a useful recurring cluster is
not flattened into one component's pooled result.

### 12.3 Cross-timeframe clusters

Test same-timeframe and cross-timeframe clusters separately. Do not assume a daily
level dominates a one-hour level. Measure whether higher-timeframe inclusion changes
reaction magnitude, speed, dwell, or persistence and whether that effect survives
matched density and width controls.

## 13. Market Scope And Portability

Use the hierarchical evidence standard:

1. **Broad top-coin finding:** supports a claim across a substantial top-coin universe;
   a seven-of-ten style rule is relevant only to this broad claim.
2. **Coin-group finding:** supports a predeclared or independently confirmed group of
   similar coins with per-member agreement and no single dominant member.
3. **Asset-specific finding:** repeats across periods and later unseen data for one
   asset, but remains explicitly restricted to that asset.

BTC may be in a class of its own. SOL, ADA, BNB, or other large platform coins may form
a plausible group only when the grouping has an independent market rationale or uses
development-only similarity information and is frozen before confirmation. BNB's
exchange/ecosystem exposure is a reason to test, not assume, its membership.

Post-result cherry-picking is not confirmation. A group noticed after viewing results
becomes a new hypothesis for a later frozen batch.

Meme coins are a valid parallel market-scope investigation because participation,
liquidity, attention, reflexivity, and event concentration may differ materially from
BTC and large platform coins. Restrict each meme-cohort investigation to the `10` most
traded eligible meme coins on the frozen venue and market type. Rank them before
outcomes are inspected by median daily quote turnover over the preceding `30`
completed UTC days, and record the meme taxonomy source, venue, market type, ranking
timestamp, turnover field, eligibility/coverage rule, and exact ranked members. Keep
that membership fixed for the whole batch. If fewer than ten ranked members have
adequate causal data, report the coverage gap; do not backfill or reorder the cohort
after reading reaction results. A later batch may refresh the ranking before its
outcomes are opened.

Different meme behaviour is a hypothesis, not a guaranteed effect. Require more than
one adequately supported member for a group claim, retain DOGE-only or other
single-asset evidence as asset-specific, and compare a meme-cohort result with suitable
non-member controls. Complete or park the frozen main-generation batches before a
meme-coin result creates a later branch.

For every retained lead report:

1. per-coin results;
2. per-period results;
3. per-timeframe results;
4. whether one coin, period, or event dominates;
5. non-member controls for group claims; and
6. leave-one-coin-out stability where sample size permits.

### 15.4 Separate Component Assessment And Later Combination

**User-approved change, 19 September 2026:** remove the mandatory joint
reaction-and-direction pass requirement. A component can qualify for its own useful
role even when the other outcome is weak or absent. Do not merely lower a joint
percentage and keep the same inappropriate gate.

Assess the predeclared question separately:

1. **Direction:** does the signed move/path prediction improve on appropriate
   majority-direction, recent-trend and same-context comparisons? Unusually high
   volume, large movement or a level-reaction pass is not required for this role.
2. **Activity, magnitude or timing:** does the input help predict volume, movement
   size, pressure change, volatility or timing? Correct signed direction is not
   required. These behaviours are separate outcomes, not a requirement that all rise.
3. **Reaction location:** does the calculated level or cluster identify a repeatable
   difference from fair locations? A direction call is not required.
4. **Conditional modifier:** does the input strengthen, suppress, delay, reverse or
   otherwise improve a specified relationship within a defensible known context?
   Broad standalone prediction of either activity or direction is not required.

Retain an input only for the role actually supported. Keep exploratory leads separate
from confirmed components. Adequate independent episodes, honest decision-time inputs,
appropriate controls, effect size, uncertainty, selection-aware checks and later
chronological repetition still matter. This change relaxes the joint objective, not
evidence quality. A positive point estimate alone is not a pass.

The earlier `55%` useful / `65%` main aspirations remain useful reference targets for
directional hit rates where that measure fits the question, assessed independently
of activity. They are not a universal pass threshold for rare-event occurrence,
continuous magnitude, location or modifier effects. A component below such a reference
target can remain an exploratory incremental lead when its comparison supports that
description; do not upgrade it to confirmed or reject it solely by the percentage.
Freeze the relevant metric and comparison before opening new outcomes. No universal
replacement joint floor is introduced.

Report distinct quantities with explicit denominators:

- reaction/activity performance for calls made before the measured outcome;
- direction accuracy among all issued directional calls, regardless of later reaction;
- direction conditional on a realised reaction, labelled as a retrospective subgroup
  unless an outcome-blind reaction gate selected it beforehand;
- joint reaction-and-direction success when the question actually asks for both;
- eligible episodes, issued calls, abstentions, coverage, uncertainty, coin/period
  concentration and effect over relevant simple controls.

Joint success remains a diagnostic for a combined claim, not a veto on either
component. Its ceiling cannot exceed either component's rate on the same denominator;
do not compare it automatically with a 50% direction-only coin-flip baseline. For
example, 60 correct directions in 100 calls and only 40 abnormal-volume episodes do
not by themselves invalidate the directional component because joint success is
below 55%; direction still needs its own fair control and confirmation.

Useful components are eligible for a bounded combination experiment, not automatic
combined acceptance. Align decision times, compare on the same eligible episodes,
count shared events once, and test each ingredient and the proposed combination.
Do not multiply hit rates, assume independence, or require a modifier to work alone.
The combination needs later confirmation of its own intended role.

Preserve historical frozen definitions, outputs and original verdicts. Add a dated
component-role reassessment alongside them; an old joint failure may contain a useful
component but is not automatically a new pass. Review joint-gated parked items before
repeating simulations. Before running an older evaluator, verify that its output is
labelled legacy joint scoring or extend the new assessment separately; never silently
use a hard-coded old joint veto as current policy. Existing frozen confirmation runs
keep their original score for comparison and may receive this separately labelled
component assessment without outcome-led threshold tuning.

`Several reliable indications` means at least three materially independent retained
components from at least two route families, confirmed for their own stated roles.
Five or more independently useful mechanisms remain preferred. Repeated settings of
one signal are not separate discoveries. Their eventual combined directional/trading
utility still needs demonstration; no component or combined score authorizes trading.

#### 15.6.2 Core Hierarchy And Market States

The new investigation separates three clocks:

1. **Slow background:** trailing weeks or months of macro, financial, geopolitical,
   crypto, liquidity, and broad-market pressure using only source-ready data.
2. **Event clock:** the anticipation, release, first report, confirmation, escalation,
   relief, implementation, or reversal of one predeclared event.
3. **Local market clock:** the first BTC, ETH, breadth, cohort, and coin-local movement;
   nearby levels or clusters; volume, pressure, compression, attention, and liquidity;
   and later settling or range failure.

Represent the path using plain market states where support permits:

1. quiet or range-bound;
2. pressure building;
3. scheduled event approaching;
4. unexpected event detected but not yet independently confirmed;
5. event confirmed while the market accepts, rejects, or conflicts with its apparent
   implication;
6. BTC, ETH, or broad-participation leadership emerging;
7. established-alt or meme transmission and amplification; and
8. continuation, reversal, or settlement into a new range.

These states are hypotheses to test, not a mandatory story imposed on every move.

The hierarchy is an information sequence, not a fixed dominance order. Slow
background, accumulated narratives, event surprise, positioning, liquidity, technical
structure, and later news may reinforce or oppose one another. The same release can
produce a strong, weak, delayed, reversed, or absent response when the dominant market
concern and surrounding pressures differ. Analyse those conditional paths rather than
averaging them into one permanent bullish, bearish, or irrelevant label.

#### 15.6.3 Layer 1 - Honest History And Frozen Definitions

Complete this whole construction layer before reading event outcomes:

1. freeze event families, eligible source blocks, availability rules, and duplicate
   handling;
2. separate scheduled events, unexpected shocks, persistent background changes, and
   structurally anticipated crypto events;
3. preserve expectations only when their historical publication and availability can
   be reconstructed; otherwise test activity but not surprise direction;
4. define BTC, ETH, and broad-participation leadership without using the final move;
5. freeze established-coin and top-ten traded meme cohorts from pre-outcome liquidity
   information;
6. define each coin's ordinary sensitivity to the market using earlier data, so raw
   meme volatility is not mistaken for event amplification;
7. define event-type-specific minute, hour, day, and where justified week grids;
8. define when a post-event range first becomes knowable; and
9. freeze matched no-event, high-news/no-confirmed-event, false-time, source-missing,
   and ordinary-market controls.

#### 15.6.4 Layer 2 - Test Every Link Separately

Finish or honestly park every sibling before pairwise work:

1. major-event information versus unusual BTC, ETH, and broad-market activity;
2. event information versus signed market direction only when expectation or
   post-release information is valid;
3. BTC versus ETH versus broad participation as the first detectable market leader;
4. leader movement versus established-coin response;
5. leader movement versus meme-cohort response;
6. meme response beyond that coin's own pre-estimated ordinary movement and volatility;
7. slow background versus the magnitude, speed, asymmetry, continuation, and reversal
   of later market responses;
8. coin-local levels, clusters, activity, compression, attention, and liquidity as
   separate response descriptions and possible modifiers; and
9. causal formation, residence, revisit, and failure of post-event ranges.

Report whether a source predicts only activity, adds signed direction, describes
simultaneous movement, or provides a usable lead-lag relationship. Do not call
simultaneous BTC and meme movement a forecast merely because BTC is named first.
Also report whether a weak standalone factor changes another influence's magnitude,
timing, path, continuation, reversal, or transmission. Standalone weakness does not
reject a declared modifier role.

#### 15.6.5 Layer 3 - Frozen Pairwise Relationships

After the complete Layer 2 review, freeze all justified materially different pairs at
once. Candidate pairs include:

1. slow background plus event surprise or event confirmation;
2. event state plus BTC or ETH position within its current range;
3. event state plus the first broad-market response;
4. market-leader movement plus established-alt or meme-cohort breadth;
5. market-leader movement plus coin-local level or independent cluster;
6. market-leader movement plus local volume, pressure, compression, or attention;
7. event or leader movement plus source-ready orderbook disturbance; and
8. post-event range position plus a smaller new event or technical reaction area.

Each pair must be tested against both components alone on identical rows and must lose
its claimed advantage when the defining event, leader, source, or local state is
shifted, stale, shuffled, or removed as appropriate. For an independent/additive
forecast claim, the pair must outperform both components. For a modifier claim, test
whether B changes A's effect within the frozen state; B need not predict the outcome
on its own. Where support permits, report neither, A-only, B-only, and A-plus-B cells.

#### 15.6.6 Layer 4 - Limited Three-Block Chains And Range Behaviour

Only retained or explicitly justified conditional components from Layer 3 may form a
three-block chain. Examples include:

1. major event plus confirmed market-leader direction plus meme-local strength;
2. slow background plus event surprise plus BTC or ETH range position;
3. market-leader movement plus broad coin participation plus local level or cluster;
4. event confirmation plus market-leader movement plus local liquidity; and
5. market-leader movement plus meme attention plus available room before an opposing
   causal area.

Do not use more than three materially distinct predictive blocks in one chain during
this layer. Remove each block in turn. Require an independent/additive chain to beat
every relevant immediately simpler version; require a modifier chain to show that the
named modifier changes the primary relationship inside the frozen context and on
later whole episodes. If many measurements describe the same underlying move, count
them as one block rather than artificial independent agreement.

Post-event range work must define boundaries only after the chosen settling rule has
become knowable. Measure boundary reactions, smaller-news ripples, range residence,
time and distance to revisit, maximum movement the wrong way, and permanent range
failure. A longer holding period is an outcome to measure, not a rescue rule for an
incorrect call.

#### 15.6.7 Layer 5 - Untouched Event And Live Confirmation

Freeze surviving simple links, pairs, and chains before testing them on later whole
events and chronological periods that did not shape their definitions. Hold out whole
events rather than random candles so one long news episode cannot masquerade as
hundreds of independent successes.

Confirmation must include, as support permits:

1. more than one event family and chronological period;
2. BTC, established-alt, and meme scopes kept separately interpretable;
3. positive and negative event outcomes under differing slow backgrounds;
4. scheduled and unexpected-event modes kept separate;
5. realistic source outages, delays, rumours, corrections, and abstentions;
6. event-only, leader-only, group-only, local-only, and immediately simpler controls;
7. reaction, signed direction, continuation/reversal, amplification, range, and joint
   success reported separately; and
8. concentration checks showing whether one event, coin, or source supplies the result.

#### 15.6.8 Branch Interpretation

Use these ordinary-language decisions after each complete layer:

1. If news predicts unusual activity but not direction, retain it as an activity or
   readiness warning and require market confirmation for direction.
2. If BTC consistently leads, test follower timing; if movement is simultaneous, retain
   BTC only as common context rather than advance evidence.
3. If ETH or broad participation leads in a defined state, retain that conditional
   leader rather than forcing BTC dominance.
4. If raw meme outperformance disappears after adjusting for the coin's normal
   sensitivity and volatility, reject the amplification claim.
5. If local conditions add nothing beyond event and leader information, retain them
   only for their separately demonstrated reaction or risk role.
6. If positive-event moves fade more often during a causally defined negative
   background, queue capped-rally and return-to-range questions; reject that asymmetry
   if it does not repeat.
7. If post-event ranges fail frequently or expose unacceptable unrecovered movement,
   reject `wait longer` as an explanation.
8. If story, expectation, orderbook, or global-source history is not reconstructable,
   restrict the claim to official scheduled events or prospective accumulation rather
   than guessing missing states.
9. If an event's average is modest but its strong, weak, delayed, or reversed episodes
   separate under a timestamp-safe rational condition, retain that condition as a
   development-only modifier lead and freeze it for later whole-event confirmation.
10. If positive information appears during a bearish background, measure whether it
    reduced the expected decline, changed the initial path, or was later overwhelmed;
    do not score only the final sign.
11. If post-event volume or pressure predicts continuation, retain it as confirmation
    of the event response. Do not reinterpret that downstream observation as a rival
    root cause or evidence that the initiating news did not matter.
12. If several minor stories align, separate genuinely distinct accumulating events
    from duplicate reporting of one story before testing narrative pressure.

This is a five-layer maximum permission, not a requirement to manufacture descendants.
Every layer remains breadth-first: complete all frozen siblings, perform one joint
review, queue inspired ideas, and only then freeze the next layer.

### 15.7 Evidence-To-Usable-Signal Portfolio - Authorized 8 September 2026

After the current frozen broad comparison is terminal and jointly reviewed, move from
open-ended source discovery toward a compact automated signal portfolio. This is an
additive hand-forward stage: it does not delete, downgrade, or conceal any retained,
conditional, specialist, unresolved, parked, or negative finding in the approved
ledgers. `Whittling down` means limiting the active implementation surface, not
rewriting the historical evidence as if only five ideas were ever found.

The intended output is not one universal mechanical trading rule. It is a coding-based
set of independently testable signal families. Different families may identify
different circumstances, coins, directions, horizons, or possible entry, hold,
reduce-risk, exit, or abstention roles and may retain different honestly measured
success rates. A family may remain useful for activity, timing, risk, or context even
when it does not independently predict signed direction.

Use these five provisional families as the organizing frame, subject to the complete
current-batch review choosing the strongest causal representation inside each one:

1. **Major-event information:** scheduled macro or financial releases, unexpected
   events, event activity, signed surprise only where prior expectation is genuinely
   available, and early post-event confirmation.
2. **Background and market leadership:** slow market pressure, BTC, ETH, broad-market
   participation, cross-market state, trend or range state, and which eligible leader
   becomes informative first.
3. **Calculated reaction areas:** isolated causal levels and genuine independent
   clusters, including their distance, approach, age, timeframe, room, conflict, and
   demonstrated reaction role.
4. **Local participation and pressure:** volume, volatility, buying/selling pressure,
   compression, momentum, liquidity, and exhaustion or acceleration around a
   separately qualified event or reaction area.
5. **Cross-asset transmission and amplification:** BTC/ETH/broad-market movement into
   established-coin or meme cohorts, including lag, breadth, ordinary-beta adjustment,
   coin-local modification, and whether the follower response is genuinely unusual.

These are containers for distinct supported mechanisms, not five mandatory bundled
models. Preserve multiple useful variants inside a family when they answer genuinely
different questions. Preserve an asset-specific or coin-group-specific signal under
its honest scope rather than discarding it for failing to generalize universally.

Every automated family output should be reason-labelled and expose, where applicable:

1. the observation time and latest contributing data time;
2. the detected event, state, level, cluster, or transmission relationship;
3. activity-only, upward, downward, continuation, reversal, or risk meaning;
4. applicable coin/group and expected horizon or expiry;
5. evidence strength, sample support, coverage, and known limitations;
6. source availability or missingness;
7. conflicting and supporting families; and
8. an explicit no-call state when evidence is absent, stale, weak, or contradictory.

Do not force every family into one opaque score. First test each family independently.
Then test only named pairwise and limited three-family relationships that are already
justified by the completed evidence. Same-direction agreement may increase confidence,
opposition may reduce confidence or support an exit/risk role, and a major event may
override a local technical indication only when those relationships survive the
required controls; none of those behaviours is assumed in advance.

The implementation sequence is:

1. complete and integrity-check the already-frozen broad FreqAI batch;
2. jointly review all results and existing retained leads, without selecting only the
   largest model scores;
3. choose the simplest causal, non-redundant representation worth implementing inside
   each of the five families and preserve all non-selected evidence in the ledgers;
4. build offline reason-labelled signal prototypes and test each against its direct
   controls, chronological unseen data, and declared market scope;
5. use FreqAI, where it adds value, to rank confidence, calibrate magnitude, or abstain
   and compare it with the readable direct representation;
6. test only evidence-backed family combinations, comparing independent/additive
   claims against every component alone and modifier claims against the primary
   relationship with and without that modifier; and
7. only after those gates, assess candidate entry/exit mappings, costs, adverse path,
   missed movement, exits, and operational behaviour under the separately routed
   entry/exit, promotion, and risk rules.

This section authorizes offline research prototypes and later evidence-backed mapping
to possible trading-action roles. It does not authorize live orders, automatic
strategy promotion, leverage, position sizing, or alteration of the verified dry-run
strategy. No paid source is required: unavailable expectation, news, orderbook, or
other context must remain explicitly unavailable or activity-only rather than being
guessed.

11. strong, weak, delayed, reversed, and absent episodes retained rather than averaged

12. machine-discovered conditional relationships translated into readable roles and

## 17. Volume Profile Refinement Programme

Volume Profile is a priority family because it exposes explicit level construction
choices and OHLCV volume-location assumptions.

Test current defaults first. Then, only when Generation 0 evidence justifies the branch,
consider:

### 17.1 Resolution

Compare fixed bin counts with rational adaptive resolution based on profile price
range, ATR, lower-timeframe observation count, or minimum effective volume per bin.

### 17.2 Nodes as connected zones

Test whether adjacent high-volume bins should form one HVN zone and adjacent
low-volume bins one LVN corridor instead of treating one bin centre as an exact level.

### 17.3 Node stability

Measure persistence across successive profiles, nearby window lengths, physical zone
width, and separation from stronger neighbours. One-candle nodes may be histogram
noise.

### 17.4 HVN and LVN definitions

Compare existing relative thresholds with defensible alternatives such as local
prominence, within-profile percentiles, connected components, or neighbourhood
contrast. Do not select a definition because it catches one known reversal.

### 17.5 Volume placement

Where lower-timeframe history exists, test higher-timeframe profiles constructed from
lower-timeframe candles against the approximation that distributes each coarse
candle's volume within its range.

### 17.6 Window construction

Keep rolling, calendar/session, and structurally anchored profiles separately labelled.
They express different market hypotheses and should not be mixed into one winning
parameter search.

### 17.7 Required interpretation

For each VP change explain:

1. what construction defect it addresses;
2. how it changes level density, width, persistence, and availability;
3. whether reaction evidence improves against same-density controls;
4. whether improvement repeats outside development data; and
5. whether added complexity is justified.

## 18. Other Indicator Refinement

The same process applies to pivots, trendlines, geometry, VWAP, structure, and any new
level family.

Before changing code:

1. identify the observed representation failure;
2. state a rational mechanism for the proposed improvement;
3. test configuration-only alternatives where possible;
4. predict the expected effect on level timing, density, width, or stability;
5. freeze development and confirmation data; and
6. define what would reject the change.

Examples of rational refinements include fixing timestamp availability, making a
height tolerance scale with market movement, representing an uncertain coordinate as

known winning event, choosing parameters from the final confirmation period, or adding
an opaque score whose components cannot be ablated.

## 20. Rules For Accepting A Refinement

A changed or new indicator survives only when:

1. the market rationale existed before confirmation testing;
2. the implementation is causal and reproducible;
3. it improves reaction evidence rather than only strategy profit;
4. it beats matched, shifted, and same-density controls;
5. the improvement is visible in effect size or calibration, not only one model metric;
6. it repeats across multiple chronological periods;
7. it supports an honest broad, group-specific, or asset-specific market scope;
8. it is not dominated by one exceptional event;
9. it survives untouched confirmation data;
10. its complexity and compute cost are justified; and
11. it does not obtain apparent improvement merely by making levels extremely rare.

A failed refinement does not invalidate the whole indicator family if the test exposed
a representation problem. Classify the result correctly and test only a rational next
representation.

## 21. Result Classes

Every completed family or branch receives one of these plain-language classifications:

1. **Repeatable reaction-zone lead:** level contact differs materially from strong
   controls and repeats under the declared market scope.
2. **Context-dependent reaction lead:** useful only under a predeclared or later
   independently confirmed condition.
3. **Cluster-specific lead:** the combination adds information beyond every component
   and matched-density controls.
4. **Activity-only lead:** volume, volatility, pressure, or timing changes, but no
   stable path distinction is established.
5. **Acceptance/traversal lead:** the level distinguishes stalling, residence, crossing,
   or fast traversal rather than a conventional bounce.
6. **Redundant level:** appears useful but adds nothing beyond a simpler level or market
   state.
7. **Density artifact:** apparent success is explained by excessive chart coverage or
   repeated contacts.
8. **Representation failure:** implementation or encoding cannot fairly test the
   underlying market idea.
9. **Insufficient coverage:** causal eligible episodes are too few for the claim.
10. **Unstable or null:** does not reproduce or does not beat controls.
11. **Conditional modifier:** weak or inconsistent alone, but reproducibly changes a
    named influence's probability, size, timing, path, or transmission under a frozen
    context.
12. **Confirmation-only lead:** usefully updates the episode after the initiating
    event, without predicting or replacing that event as the driver.
13. **Amplified, suppressed, delayed, or reversed response:** the influence is visible
    but its realised path changes across declared surrounding conditions.
14. **Dominant-override candidate:** one sufficiently important influence repeatedly
    overwhelms several ordinarily relevant background or technical conditions.
15. **Unexplained episode difference:** contrasting responses remain meaningful, but
    no timestamp-safe conditional explanation has yet reproduced.

None of these classes is an entry, exit, long, short, or live-trading promotion.
