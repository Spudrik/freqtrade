# Reference - FreqAI Event-Reaction Research Method

## 1. Status And Required Use

This is a detailed starting reference for agents investigating whether a trader-readable level, event, market state, or interaction is associated with a repeatable change in subsequent market behaviour.

It preserves reusable methods developed during earlier event-reaction research. It is not a complete guide to every FreqAI use case and it does not replace the active objective, task-specific manifest, direct-test rules, promotion rules, or runtime rules.

Under current Objective 02b, causal levels and level clusters remain a research
surface, and the three frozen fresh-confirmation questions remain unchanged. The user
also authorized a five-layer event-driven hierarchy on 3 September 2026: slow
background -> major event -> BTC/ETH/broad-market confirmation -> established-alt or
meme response -> coin-local modification -> continuation, reversal, or post-event
range. Sieve entries/exits are not required.

Signed direction is permitted only inside predeclared event, market-confirmation,
coin-group, coin-local, post-event-range, or evidence-triggered `1m` questions.
Unrestricted every-candle direction, trade actions, profit optimization, and live or
dry-run changes remain outside the objective.

Read it when a routed task asks FreqAI to investigate any of:

1. exact entry or exit events;
2. level proximity, overlap, conflict, confluence, or precedence;
3. price reaction magnitude, direction, path, timing, volume, pressure, or volatility;
4. whether an exact trigger adds information beyond an already-active market state;
5. whether an exit reason adds information beyond observable trade state;
6. whether an effect is broad-market, coin-group-specific, or asset-specific; or
7. whether two or more signals interact rather than merely duplicate one another;
8. whether a timestamp-safe major event changes BTC, ETH, or broad-market behaviour;
9. whether a market leader transmits movement to established coins or memes;
10. whether a meme response is unusual after allowing for its ordinary volatility and
    market sensitivity; or
11. whether an event move settles into a new causally measurable range.

## 2. Central Research Question

Every experiment must begin with a plain trader question. A general form is:

> Given the complete information available at the decision time, how does this exact
> event, level, state, or interaction change the probability, size, timing, order, or
> direction of the next market response relative to what comparable market states
> would otherwise imply?

Do not begin with `find the best model`, `find the most profitable horizon`, or `run every feature`. FreqAI is the controlled nonlinear testing tool, not the source of the market question.

## 3. Plain-Language Vocabulary

Use these meanings consistently:

1. **Event:** the safe timestamp at which a technical condition becomes true or a
   scheduled/unscheduled external development first becomes available in its declared
   report, confirmation, or implementation state.
2. **Active state:** the condition remains present after its first event. An exact trigger and an already-active episode are different claims.
3. **Reaction:** a change in movement size, speed, volume, pressure, volatility, path, or direction after the decision time.
4. **Activity:** greater absolute movement, volume, volatility, or faster threshold contact without a proven signed direction.
5. **Direction:** upward/downward or long-favourable/short-favourable path information. Activity is not direction.
6. **Path:** what happened between the decision time and the final horizon, including which favourable or adverse point occurred first.
7. **Horizon:** a predeclared future observation distance such as `1h`, `2h`, `4h`, `8h`, `12h`, `24h`, or `48h`.
8. **Control:** a fair comparison that lacks the exact ingredient being tested while keeping the other relevant conditions the same.
9. **Placebo:** deliberately irrelevant information, such as the event or level state shifted seven days into the past, used to check whether the live information really matters.
10. **Ablation:** removing one feature block from a model to determine whether that block carried unique information.
11. **Market class or coin group:** a frozen set of assets with a trader-readable or measurable similarity that was defined without using the future outcomes being tested.
12. **Association:** the event and response occurred together more often than in controls. This does not establish that the event caused the response.
13. **Event surprise:** the actual outcome relative to an expectation that was
    genuinely published or measurable beforehand; it is not the headline's tone.
14. **Market leader:** BTC, ETH, or broad participation that moved first under a frozen
    early-window rule; simultaneous or conflicting movement means no stable leader.
15. **Transmission:** movement in an established-alt or meme cohort following the
    declared market response.
16. **Amplification:** coin or cohort movement beyond its earlier-estimated ordinary
    market sensitivity, lag, and volatility; a larger raw percentage move is not
    sufficient.
17. **Post-event range:** a range whose boundaries become knowable only after a frozen
    settling rule has completed.
18. **Abstention:** an explicit no-call outcome caused by missing or conflicting event,
    source, expectation, leader, or model evidence.
19. **Background condition:** information accumulated before the immediate trigger
    that changes the market's starting pressure, likely range, or sensitivity.
20. **Driver or trigger:** new information or a newly active condition that can alter
    the balance of market pressure.
21. **Confirmation:** a response observed after a possible driver that shows whether
    participants noticed, accepted, rejected, or conflicted over it. Confirmation is
    not an alternative root cause merely because it predicts the later path.
22. **Modifier:** a condition that amplifies, suppresses, reverses, delays, shortens,
    gates, or blocks transmission of another influence without needing a strong
    standalone average effect.
23. **Accumulator:** several genuinely distinct smaller influences whose rolling
    combination may matter even when no individual item has a stable standalone
    effect.
24. **Override:** a sufficiently important new influence that temporarily dominates
    several ordinarily useful background or technical conditions.
25. **Expected path:** the response distribution implied by comparable timestamp-safe
    pre-event states, not a claim that one exact counterfactual future is knowable.
26. **Whole episode:** one connected sequence from background and expectation through
    trigger, initial response, market transmission, local modification, and later
    continuation, reversal, or settlement.

### 3.1 Whole-Episode Conditional Interpretation Protocol

The market is a changing balance of influences, not a collection of universal
standalone rules. A finding should answer `under what surrounding conditions did this
information change the expected path?`, not only `was the final candle up or down?`.

For every event or level episode, preserve these timestamp-safe layers where data
permits:

1. long-running macro, financial, geopolitical, liquidity, and crypto background;
2. the rolling balance of distinct positive, negative, and conflicting narratives;
3. the contemporaneous expectation and information already reflected in price;
4. the event's novelty, credibility, severity, scope, and surprise;
5. the initial BTC, ETH, or broad-market volume, pressure, volatility, and signed
   response;
6. technical structure, causal levels or clusters, positioning, and liquidity that
   may alter that response;
7. established-coin, meme-cohort, and coin-local transmission; and
8. the response path across time, including amplification, suppression, delay,
   continuation, reversal, and range formation.

Assign every tested field a role at the exact decision time: background, possible
driver, confirmation, modifier, accumulator, duplicate/substitute, or outcome. The
same field may change roles when the decision time changes. For example, volume before
a release can describe anticipation, volume immediately after it can confirm the news
response, and later volume can be the outcome being forecast.

Do not condition on a consequence of an event and then interpret the event's reduced
model contribution as evidence that the consequence, rather than the event, was the
root cause. It is valid to use an initial response to predict a later response, but
that is a later-decision confirmation question and must not be reported as a
pre-event forecast or causal replacement.

Measure both the raw outcome and the change from an appropriate expected path. A
positive influence can be meaningful when it reduces an otherwise likely fall; a
negative influence can matter when it caps an otherwise likely rise. Preserve initial
impulse, strongest move, time to peak, transmission, fade, reversal, and later regime
resumption rather than compressing the episode into one terminal sign.

Consider at least these interaction roles where the market theory supports them:

1. amplifier;
2. suppressor;
3. reverser;
4. necessary or enabling gate;
5. accumulator of distinct small influences;
6. dominant override;
7. delay or shortening effect;
8. transmission blocker; and
9. substitute or duplicate measurement.

A field does not need to predict the outcome alone to remain a rational modifier. On
development data, compare strong, weak, delayed, reversed, and absent responses to
form conditional hypotheses. Freeze each discovered condition before later whole-
event confirmation. This contrastive analysis is required to learn from apparent
failures, but it must not become outcome-led storytelling.

Where support permits, compare neither ingredient, A only, B only, and A plus B on
comparable rows. For a standalone or additive forecast claim, the larger model must
beat the relevant simpler models. For a modifier claim, test whether B changes A's
effect within the declared context; do not require B to have a broad standalone
effect. Report activation frequency and cell support so a rare combination cannot
look dependable from a few episodes.

The effect of an event family may change because the dominant market concern,
expectation, positioning, liquidity, surrounding narrative, or technical structure
changed. Preserve those differences rather than averaging them away. Any context
partition discovered after opening outcomes remains provisional until frozen and
reproduced on untouched events.

## 4. Bounded Research Sequence

Use this sequence unless the active objective defines a stricter one:

1. State the accepted facts that this test must not redundantly revisit.
2. State the decision time and the baseline expected path without the proposed
   ingredient.
3. State one exact hypothesis in trader-readable language, including whether the
   ingredient is proposed as a driver, confirmation, modifier, accumulator, or
   outcome.
4. State what different evidence decision would follow from a positive, negative, or
   unresolved result. Do not run a test that cannot change the evidence map.
5. Define the event, eligible rows, feature blocks, outcomes, controls, horizons,
   coins, periods, and pass/fail or classification rules before inspecting the result.
6. Run direct event-versus-control and conditional-state comparisons first where
   feasible.
7. Run the smallest FreqAI model ladder that can distinguish the proposed explanation
   from its alternatives.
8. Compare every model with the immediately simpler models required for the exact
   standalone, additive, or modifier claim.
9. Separate broad-market, coin-group, asset-specific, side-specific, timeframe-
   specific, and background-specific evidence.
10. Classify the result as a lead, conditional lead, modifier, confirmation,
    unresolved, not reproduced, parked, candidate edge, or promotable according to
    the routed rules.
11. Complete every batch in the active generation before launching result-driven
    branches.
12. Stop when the frozen question is answered or its iteration limit is reached. Do
    not rescue a failed representation through unplanned features, horizons,
    thresholds, or groups.

Capture useful ideas from interim results in the rolling branch queue, but do not treat
capture as execution permission. New indicators, mathematics, timeframes, level or
cluster definitions, coins, and contextual blocks wait for the complete generation
review and a frozen later batch.

## 5. Freeze The Data Surface

Before a run, record:

1. exact pairs and market type;
2. source ranking/date when a top-coin universe is used;
3. base candle timeframe and informative timeframes;
4. available date range for every pair/timeframe;
5. chronological development, validation, and sealed holdout windows;
6. known gaps, duplicate timestamps, and missing source blocks;
7. snapshot or cache paths and hashes where the approved harness supports them;
8. decision-time boundary and higher-timeframe candle alignment;
9. event eligibility mask and its digest;
10. outcome horizons and target definitions; and
11. model class, parameters, training cadence, identifiers, and output paths.

Use only information available at the decision time. For a higher timeframe, use the most recently completed candle, record its age, and never use a candle that was still forming.

## 6. Build A Traceable Event Catalogue

For every technical level, entry, or exit event row, preserve enough information to
reconstruct why it exists:

1. timestamp and pair;
2. long/short or supporting/opposing orientation;
3. exact source strategy, entry family, exit family, and reason;
4. native and source timeframes;
5. exact onset versus already-active state;
6. level identity, price, distance, age, touch count, and most recent interaction;
7. nearby same-direction and opposing levels;
8. partial/final/warning/tightening/runner action role for exits;
9. originating trade and observable trade state where relevant; and
10. parameter source and Sieve traceability.

Do not silently merge exact reasons into broad families. A broad family may pool reasons with opposite future paths.

For every external or scheduled-event record, preserve where genuinely available:

1. event family, entities, markets, and affected geography;
2. scheduled versus unexpected status;
3. scheduled time or first safe public-availability timestamp;
4. expectation value and its time/source, or explicit expectation unavailability;
5. actual outcome and surprise relative to the prior expectation;
6. first report, independent-source confirmation, official confirmation,
   implementation, correction, escalation, and relief timestamps;
7. story novelty, persistence, source diversity, entity diversity, and duplicate
   handling;
8. source presence, completeness, freshness, and maximum contributing timestamp;
9. slow-background state constructed only from earlier data;
10. BTC, ETH, and broad-market candidate-leader states at the frozen early checkpoints;
11. eligible established-alt and meme cohorts frozen before outcomes; and
12. the event-type-specific observation grid and control timestamps.

Do not select `major` events from later price movement. Famous events may verify
interpretability, but the scored surface must include every event meeting the frozen
category and availability rules.

## 7. Measure Behaviour As A Path

Forward return at one chosen candle is only a baseline. Use the target families required by the trader question.

### 7.1 Reaction magnitude and timing

Suitable measurements include:

1. maximum absolute movement normalized by ATR;
2. whether price touched `0.5`, `1`, or another predeclared ATR threshold;
3. which future candle first touched the threshold;
4. time-to-touch with non-touching cases retained rather than discarded;
5. range expansion; and
6. continuation or rejection after first contact.

Example: if recent ATR is `$2`, movement from `$100` to either `$102` or `$98` is a one-ATR reaction. This proves movement, not direction.

### 7.2 Signed direction and path

Suitable measurements include:

1. signed return;
2. upward versus downward excursion;
3. long-favourable or short-favourable excursion;
4. path balance between favourable and adverse movement;
5. target-before-invalidation;
6. favourable-before-adverse ordering;
7. breakout-before-rejection or rejection-before-breakout; and
8. terminal direction kept separate from the route taken to reach it.

Example: after a long exit at `$100`, a move to `$103` before `$97` is favourable-before-adverse. A move to `$97` before `$103` is adverse-before-favourable even if both paths later close at the same price.

### 7.3 Volume, pressure, and volatility

Measure independently:

1. future volume relative to a trailing baseline;
2. buying/selling pressure and pressure change;
3. realised volatility and range expansion;
4. acceleration or weakening; and
5. whether activity changes without a consistent signed response.

Do not relabel higher volume, faster movement, or greater volatility as bullish or bearish evidence.

### 7.4 Evidence-triggered one-minute replay

Use `1m` data as a microscope only after a higher-timeframe analysis has defined a
repeatable direction-neutral reaction pattern. Do not search all historical candles
for attractive directional examples.

For each queued episode:

1. freeze selection from direction-neutral facts before reading its `1m` signed path;
2. preserve the causal level/cluster, source timeframe, contact time, zone width,
   approach side, and every independently qualifying episode;
3. scale the dense replay envelope to the causal anchor: normally `24h/12h`
   pre/post for `1h`, `72h/48h` for `4h`, `7d/4d` for `8h`, and `30d/14d` for `1d`;
4. measure signed displacement, upward and downward excursion, first-passage order,
   breakout/rejection order, zone reclaim, dwell, recrossing, and persistence;
5. separately measure rolling volume, volume acceleration, candle-pressure proxies,
   momentum, volatility, and liquidity so activity is not mistaken for direction;
6. compare matched `1m` noncontacts, shifted/stale locations, and the same regime/path
   without the current level ingredient; and
7. keep retrospective price references separate from levels that were genuinely
   knowable at the time.

For clusters, the default anchor is the highest timeframe among independent
components. Load the level's full calculation history at its source timeframe in
addition to the dense minute envelope. If a direction-neutral boundary audit shows
the approach began before the window or the episode/activity remains unsettled at its
end, expand the complete pattern once rather than extending selected winners. Follow
the detailed window, target-grid, acquisition, and later-decision rules in
`theory_freqai_regime_direction_level_reaction.md`.

An initial replay is a direct aligned-path study. Do not fit FreqAI to a handful of
selected episodes. When support becomes adequate, use the frozen ladder in
`theory_freqai_regime_direction_level_reaction.md`: pre-contact `1m` path, higher-
timeframe regime, current level state, explicit interaction, placebo, and optional
source-ready orderbook/flow blocks. No one-minute result is a trading instruction.

### 7.5 Event-driven market hierarchy

Measure each link separately before combining it:

1. event availability and confirmation;
2. unusual BTC, ETH, or broad-market activity;
3. signed first response, continuation, and reversal;
4. leader identity, lead time, and simultaneous/conflicting movement;
5. established-alt and meme-cohort direction, delay, breadth, and magnitude;
6. coin response beyond its ordinary pre-event market sensitivity and volatility;
7. incremental local level, cluster, volume, pressure, compression, attention, and
   liquidity effects; and
8. time to settle, post-event range availability, residence, revisit, and failure.

Scheduled events may have a pre-release activity question and a separate post-release
direction question. Unexpected events begin no earlier than their first safe report.
Scale measurement windows to the event type: minutes to days for fast releases or
shocks, days to weeks for persistent changes, and weeks to months for structurally
anticipated events when supported.

Treat one continuing event as one dependent episode. Hold out whole events during
confirmation instead of randomly distributing their candles across training and test
sets.

### 7.6 Exit counterfactuals

Keep these meanings separate:

1. **Hold-instead result:** what the position would have gained or lost if it remained open to the horizon.
2. **Missed additional profit:** favourable movement available after exit.
3. **Avoided loss:** adverse movement after exit that the exit escaped.
4. **Net exit regret:** missed favourable movement minus avoided adverse movement under the frozen definition.
5. **Favourable/adverse peak timing:** when each side of the future path reached its extreme.
6. **Favourable-before-adverse:** which side occurred first.

An exit may miss profit and still be protective. A protective result does not imply perfect timing.

## 8. Establish The Base Market Information

The comparison model should already know the ordinary market information relevant to the hypothesis. A sensible timestamp-safe base may include:

1. OHLCV changes and candle structure;
2. position within recent ranges;
3. distance to prior highs and lows;
4. breaks above/below prior ranges;
5. ATR and range expansion;
6. relative volume;
7. directional pressure;
8. trend direction and trend strength kept separate; and
9. a compact fixed indicator layer such as RSI, Bollinger position/width, MACD, SMA/EMA distance and slope, ADX, and realised volatility.

Use the event's native timeframe, one adjacent higher timeframe, and one structural timeframe first. Add more only as a controlled comparison. Closely adjacent timeframes may repeat the same price history and must not be counted as independent agreement without testing that question.

Market-wide features such as BTC direction, ETH direction, top-coin breadth, dispersion, correlation, and decoupling may be used only when their timestamp-safe construction is frozen. News, macro, orderbook, or liquidity data may be called absent or unobserved, never quiet or neutral, until their own readiness rules pass.

## 9. Direct Tests Before FreqAI Interpretation

Use the controls relevant to the exact question:

1. ordinary eligible candles from the same clean period;
2. same market regime without the trigger;
3. matched visible-state candles without the event;
4. no-nearby-level candles for level research;
5. near-miss rows that almost triggered;
6. opposite-direction setups;
7. an exact event shifted to another timestamp;
8. current level state versus a stale level state, such as `168h` old;
9. exact onset versus an already-active episode;
10. precursor state before the trigger;
11. shuffled labels where useful as a noise check;
12. matched still-open trades for exit research; and
13. earlier, later, and no-exit counterfactuals when they can be reconstructed honestly;
14. matched no-event times under the same slow background;
15. high-news-activity times without an independently confirmed major event;
16. similar BTC or broad-market movement without the named event;
17. the event without a confirming early market response;
18. the same coin-local condition without leader movement;
19. deliberately false event times, candidate leaders, or group membership; and
20. whole-event concentration and leave-one-event-out checks;
21. the same event family or trigger under contrasting predeclared slow backgrounds;
22. comparable pre-event states with and without the trigger;
23. raw outcome versus change from the matched expected path;
24. neither ingredient, A only, B only, and A-plus-B cells for a named interaction
    when support permits;
25. strong, weak, delayed, reversed, and absent response contrasts; and
26. duplicate-source coverage versus genuinely distinct concurrent-story counts.

Report the event and control sample counts, effect size, sign, and coin/window distribution. A pooled average can hide equal and opposite groups.

Choose controls according to the claim. When estimating an event's total influence,
do not match or control on volume, pressure, price movement, or another response that
occurred because of that event. When asking a later decision-time question such as
whether the first `5m` response predicts the following `15-60m`, the initial response
may be an input, but the conclusion must be labelled confirmation or continuation,
not evidence that the event was unimportant.

## 10. FreqAI Model Ladder

Build only the layers required to answer the question. A reusable ladder is:

1. **Price/indicator baseline:** ordinary market information without the exact event or level.
2. **Generic event model:** whether any event occurred and its broad action role.
3. **Exact identity model:** exact entry, exit, family, or reason identity.
4. **Active-state model:** the condition is already active, without exact onset identity.
5. **Component models:** counts, distance, relationship, timeframe, volume, pressure, structure, or other named blocks tested separately.
6. **Combined additive model:** retained components supplied together but without claiming interaction.
7. **Explicit interaction model:** only predeclared relationships such as regime direction x level side or approach pressure x level freshness.
8. **Trade-state model:** observable position state without exit identity.
9. **Identity-plus-state model:** used only to ask whether identity adds beyond state.
10. **Stale or shifted placebo models:** the same feature representation at an irrelevant timestamp.
11. **Ablations:** remove each required ingredient in turn.

A larger model must beat both the baseline and every immediately simpler model
required by its exact claim. Beating OHLCV alone does not prove that an interaction
matters. A weak standalone modifier is not automatically excluded: for a frozen
modifier hypothesis, compare how the primary influence changes with and without that
modifier on comparable eligible rows, then confirm the conditional effect on later
whole episodes.

For the Objective 02b event hierarchy, use this attributable variant:

1. matched OHLCV and slow-background baseline without the event;
2. event-only model without the early market response;
3. BTC-only, ETH-only, and broad-participation candidate-leader models;
4. event plus the frozen leader representation;
5. group-transmission model without coin-local modifiers;
6. coin-local level/activity/attention/liquidity model without event or leader context;
7. the complete frozen pairwise portfolio;
8. limited models containing no more than three retained predictive blocks;
9. one-at-a-time removal of every claimed block; and
10. later whole-event confirmation with realistic source gaps and abstention.

An event model that merely recognizes a large movement already happening is a market-
state description, not proof of event prediction or exploitable leader-to-group delay.

## 11. Fair Model Comparison

Compared profiles must use:

1. the same eligible timestamps;
2. the same pairs and chronological windows;
3. the same target definitions and horizons;
4. the same FreqAI model class and frozen parameters unless model class itself is the hypothesis;
5. the same training cadence and feature timing;
6. the same missing-data rules;
7. comparable prediction coverage; and
8. unique, traceable model identifiers.

Audit shared keys and eligibility-mask digests before comparing reused and new prediction profiles. Never compare one model on easier rows or a different date surface and call the difference feature value.

## 12. Hierarchical Cross-Coin Evidence

Do not force every valid relationship to work across seven of ten coins. Use three distinct claim levels.

### 12.1 Broad-market claim

This means the relationship is intended to generalize across the frozen top-coin universe. A default such as at least seven suitable coins and at least two chronological windows remains reasonable for this broad claim.

Failure at this level means `not broad across the tested universe`. It does not automatically reject coherent coin-group or asset-specific behaviour.

### 12.2 Coin-group claim

This means the relationship is asserted only for a frozen group of measurably or economically similar assets.

A group-specific lead may be retained when:

1. the grouping was defined before reading the tested outcomes, or was discovered on development data and then frozen for later confirmation;
2. every member has adequate event and target support;
3. the effect has the same trader-readable meaning and sign across a clear majority of the group;
4. the relationship repeats in at least two chronological windows;
5. pooled results are not dominated by one member;
6. the group model beats the same relevant controls inside that group; and
7. the conclusion is labelled with the exact group rather than described as a general crypto edge.

For a two-coin group, both coins should agree before calling it a group lead. For a three-coin group, two agreeing coins may justify a provisional lead, but all-three agreement or later reproduction provides much stronger evidence. Exact support rules must be frozen in the batch manifest.

### 12.3 Asset-specific claim

BTC may behave differently because it is the principal crypto market driver, has different liquidity, and often supplies rather than follows wider crypto direction. A BTC-only relationship may therefore remain a valid BTC-specific lead.

It must reproduce across multiple chronological periods, adjacent sensible thresholds, and preferably later unseen data. It must never be described as a cross-crypto edge merely because BTC is important.

The same rule applies to another individual asset: use `asset_specific_lead`, not a general conclusion.

## 13. Defining Similar Coin Groups Without Cherry-Picking

Possible similarity dimensions include:

1. rolling return correlation;
2. beta and response lag relative to BTC or a top-coin basket;
3. realised volatility and drawdown profile;
4. liquidity and turnover;
5. market-cap/liquidity tier;
6. trend persistence and mean-reversion behaviour;
7. reaction to broad risk-on/risk-off moves;
8. ecosystem or economic role, when that classification has a genuine market rationale; and
9. stability of the proposed grouping through time.

SOL, ADA, and BNB might be proposed as a large-alt or layer-one-like research group, but that similarity must not be assumed. BNB has exchange-linked and ecosystem-specific behaviour, while SOL and ADA may have different volatility and beta. Define the grouping rule numerically or economically before testing its event outcomes.

Use one of these valid paths:

1. **Predeclared group:** define membership and rationale before running the outcome test.
2. **Feature-only discovery:** form groups from past returns, volatility, liquidity, or other input behaviour without using future target outcomes, then freeze them.
3. **Outcome-discovered candidate:** if similar results reveal a possible group, label it post-hoc, do not count the same result as confirmation, and test the frozen group in a later window or generation.

Do not repeatedly regroup coins until a positive combination appears. If many grouping schemes are explored, record the search breadth and require independent confirmation.

If group membership is dynamic, calculate it only from information available before each decision time. Full-sample future correlation must not decide historical group membership.

Useful confirmation checks include:

1. leave-one-coin-out analysis;
2. checking each member separately as well as pooled;
3. comparing the group with a matched non-member group;
4. testing whether group identity adds beyond each coin's own trend and BTC beta;
5. checking whether membership remains stable across periods; and
6. testing whether an apparent group result is actually one shared market episode.

### 13.1 Frozen Top-10 Meme Cohort

Meme-coin work is restricted to the `10` most traded eligible meme coins for the
selected venue and market type. Before looking at event outcomes:

1. freeze an independently sourced meme taxonomy;
2. rank eligible members by median daily quote turnover over the preceding `30`
   completed UTC days;
3. record source, venue, market type, ranking timestamp, turnover field, exact ranks,
   and the causal-data eligibility rule;
4. keep the ten members fixed for the complete batch; and
5. report missing or inadequate member coverage instead of replacing a member after
   outcomes are known.

A later batch may refresh the ranking before opening its outcomes. The expected
difference between meme coins and other markets is a testable cohort hypothesis, not
a result. Report each member, leave-one-coin-out stability where feasible, and matched
non-meme controls. A single-member result remains asset-specific.

For event-driven amplification, use earlier data to freeze each member's ordinary
response magnitude, lag, and volatility relative to BTC, ETH, or the broad basket.
Report raw movement and extra movement beyond that ordinary relationship separately.
Do not call the cohort amplified merely because memes normally have larger percentage
moves.

## 14. Metric Meanings

Do not report a model metric without translating it back to the trader question.

1. **Event-rate difference:** how many percentage points more or less often the outcome occurred than in the control.
2. **Median effect:** the middle coin/window effect; report positive and negative cells as well so the median cannot hide contradictions.
3. **Spearman rank relationship:** whether stronger model scores generally correspond to stronger outcomes, without assuming a straight-line relationship.
4. **Mean absolute error:** the typical size of the model's numeric prediction error. Lower is better.
5. **AUC:** how often a positive case is ranked above a negative case. It does not show profitability or probability calibration.
6. **Average precision:** whether the model's highest-ranked rows contain more of a rare event than the base rate.
7. **Brier error:** the squared error of predicted probabilities. Lower error means probabilities are better calibrated.
8. **Top-bucket enrichment:** whether the model's highest-confidence rows actually contain a larger or better-aligned response.
9. **Window stability:** whether the sign and useful size repeat through time rather than appearing in one period.
10. **Reaction success rate:** the share of issued calls for which the frozen abnormal
    volume-response definition is met against its causal baseline and controls.
11. **Conditional direction success:** among calls where the declared reaction really
    occurred, the share whose signed direction or path prediction was correct.
12. **Joint reaction-and-direction success:** the share of issued calls for which both
    the declared volume reaction and direction/path prediction were correct. Either
    failure makes the joint call wrong.
13. **Issued-call coverage:** the share and count of eligible independent episodes on
    which the system actually made a call; abstentions are reported separately.
14. **Direction success on issued calls:** the share of all issued directional calls
    with the correct signed/path outcome, whether or not abnormal activity followed.

For every retained result, report effect size, observations, eligible coins,
positive/negative coins, eligible windows, positive/negative windows, controls beaten,
controls failed, whether one asset dominates, and uncertainty. For joint calls, report
reaction-only, direction on all issued directional calls, conditional-direction,
joint success where relevant, issued-call coverage, and abstentions together. Do not
select future reacting cases and present their directional accuracy as an advance gate.

The user-approved 19 September 2026 change removes the blanket joint pass requirement.
Use Objective 02b Section 15.4 as the single authority: direction, activity, location,
timing/magnitude and conditional-modifier components can qualify separately. A low
joint rate does not veto a supported component. The 55%/65% directional aspirations
are not universal thresholds for other kinds of effect. Keep controls, adequate
independent support, uncertainty and later confirmation; joint rates still matter for
claims explicitly asking for both outcomes. Test combinations on common episodes
rather than multiplying component hit rates or assuming independence. Preserve old
frozen results and add dated component reassessments instead of rewriting history.

## 15. Interpretation Rules

1. Activity is not direction.
2. Prediction is not causation.
3. A profitable Sieve result is a selected starting point, not independent confirmation.
4. An exact trigger may be a timing event, a regime marker, an already-active episode marker, or a redundant description. Test those alternatives.
5. Higher timeframe receives no automatic precedence.
6. Several nearby signals may be independent confluence, duplicate descriptions, or conflict. Test component models and explicit interactions.
7. A pooled family may hide opposite reason effects. Inspect reasons before assigning one action meaning.
8. A weak broad result may contain a coherent cluster-specific effect. Test it honestly rather than either discarding it or inventing a group after the fact.
9. A failed feature representation rejects that representation in the tested scope, not every possible version of the market theory.
10. Do not blame unexplained variation on news or call periods quiet until relevant context sources are ready.
11. An isolated level and a level cluster are co-equal candidate findings. Do not
    require confluence before retaining a repeatable single-level lead, and do not
    discard a repeated cluster composition merely because its components are weak in
    an undifferentiated pooled average.
12. A cluster-pattern claim requires the exact components contacted in each episode.
    Choose an anchor component from pre-reaction information and compare the
    multi-component episodes with matched anchor-only episodes. The same future path
    cannot serve as two independent observations merely because it is described once
    as a cluster and once as a component.
13. Meme coins use the frozen top-10 traded cohort in Section 13.1. Expecting different
    behaviour is a hypothesis to test, not permission to assume a positive result or
    repeatedly regroup assets after seeing outcomes.
14. Do not ask only whether an ingredient has a positive average. Ask how it changed
    the path expected from the complete pre-event state and whether it amplified,
    suppressed, reversed, delayed, shortened, gated, accumulated with, or was
    overwhelmed by other influences.
15. A final move against an event's apparent pressure is not automatically evidence
    that the event had no effect. Compare movement size and path with suitable matched
    states; the influence may have reduced a fall, capped a rise, created only an
    initial impulse, or been overridden later.
16. The same event family can have different market meaning when the dominant concern,
    expectation, positioning, liquidity, or surrounding narrative changes. Report the
    context rather than imposing a permanent bullish or bearish label.
17. A weak standalone factor may remain a modifier or gate when that role is
    trader-readable and later confirmed. Do not demand universal standalone strength
    from a factor whose declared purpose is conditional.
18. Do not count duplicated articles, correlated indicators, adjacent timeframes, or
    several coins reacting to one common shock as independent agreement. Preserve
    genuine breadth while counting the underlying episode once for confirmation.
19. Every interpretation must separate observation, proposed explanation, plausible
    alternative, evidence strength, failure conditions, and next decision.

## 16. Batch-Gated Branching

When the active objective uses research generations:

1. freeze every batch in the current generation;
2. finish all batches before allowing results to redirect the work;
3. record possible broad, group-specific, inverse, sparse, or asset-specific branches while the generation runs;
4. review all completed results together;
5. select only questions with a clear evidence basis and alternative explanation;
6. freeze the next generation before execution; and
7. do not branch merely because unused branch depth is available.

Use a provenance-linked rolling queue so useful ideas are not lost while the frozen
scope finishes. Each item records the source result, plain-language observation,
method family, exact hypothesis, alternative explanation/control, required data,
smallest useful test, cost, earliest eligible generation, and status. Interim-result
ideas remain provisional until the parent generation is terminal. Deduplicate at the
joint review, then freeze one balanced later batch; results from that batch queue work
for the following review rather than launching recursive descendants.

While Objective 02b remains unresolved, assess at least five materially distinct route
families and eventually give at least five a fair terminal test. Suitable families are
isolated levels; level clusters/convergence; rational alternative level mathematics;
multi-timeframe regime/path/obstacle interactions; frozen coin/cohort portability;
evidence-triggered `1m` behaviour; and source-ready orderbook, flow, news, or global
context. The current hierarchy additionally covers slow background, major-event state,
market leadership, group transmission, genuine meme amplification, local modification,
and post-event ranges. Five cosmetic variants do not count, and this breadth
requirement is separate from the branch horizon currently authorized in the active
objective.

Permission-gated work follows the same bounded discipline. Number each outstanding
permission request in user updates. Pause only the mutation or scope expansion that
needs approval; continue independent safe work within the active objective when a
useful path remains. Never treat silence as permission.

A newly noticed SOL/ADA/BNB pattern during an active batch is a later candidate, not permission to rerun the current batch on those coins immediately.

Objective 02b now permits its five-layer event-driven hierarchy as the main forward
sequence. The evidence-triggered `1m` replay remains a bounded diagnostic side lane,
not a branching exception. Its result cannot spawn a descendant, alter the main
generation, or tune a parent indicator until the complete generation review.

## 19. Worked Interpretation Patterns

### 19.1 Aggregate level reaction but failed representation

Suppose current nearby levels are followed by a four-percentage-point higher chance of a one-ATR move than matched no-level candles, but only six of ten coins are positive and a seven-day-old level model performs similarly.

Correct conclusion:

> This level arrangement is associated with somewhat greater activity in aggregate, but the current level fields do not yet provide a broad, independent FreqAI input. Check predeclared coin groups or later data only through a new frozen test.

Incorrect conclusions:

1. the level causes price to move;
2. the level predicts direction;
3. the result is useless because it missed seven of ten; or
4. select the six positive coins after seeing the result and call that confirmation.

### 19.2 Generic exit flag with opposite reason effects

Suppose a generic `exit happened` model improves path-order prediction, but maximum-hold exits are followed by adverse-first movement while stop-loss exits are followed by favourable-first movement.

Correct conclusion:

> Exit occurrence marks a changed trade/market state, but the pooled flag has no single action meaning. Separate reasons and require each reason to beat trade-state and shifted-identity controls.

Incorrect conclusion:

> The exit family is good or bad.

### 19.3 BTC-only response

Suppose a level feature repeats on BTC in three periods but not on altcoins.

Correct conclusion:

> This is a BTC-specific lead. Test whether BTC's liquidity, market-leader role, volatility state, or regime explains it, and confirm on later unseen BTC data.

Incorrect conclusion:

> This is a general crypto edge.

### 19.4 News trigger and volume confirmation

Suppose activity becomes exceptional immediately after a timestamped macro release,
and unusually high first-hour volume also predicts that activity remains high for the
next several hours.

Correct conclusion:

> The release is the initiating event; first-hour volume measures and confirms the
> strength of the market response; volume persistence may help forecast how long the
> active episode continues. Test the event's total response against matched no-event
> periods, and test continuation from the later decision time separately.

Incorrect conclusion:

> Once current volume is included, the event adds little, so volume rather than the
> news caused the activity.

### 19.5 Positive pressure inside a negative background

Suppose a positive surprise is followed by a small decline, while matched episodes
with the same pre-event negative background normally decline much further. The market
initially rises, fails at resistance, and then resumes the broader decline.

Correct conclusion:

> The event supplied positive short-term pressure but did not overcome the background
> and obstacle. It reduced the expected loss and changed the initial path; resistance
> or later negative pressure may have suppressed and reversed the response. Freeze
> those possible modifiers and test them on later whole events.

Incorrect conclusions:

1. the positive event had no effect because the final return was negative; or
2. resistance caused the reversal merely because the paths coincided once.
