# Objective Examples

Use these examples to preserve the intended nuance. Do not collapse them into a single indicator, single timeframe, or single source.

## Example 1: Bearish Event Breakdown Confluence

Theory: downside continuation risk rises when technical resistance, orderbook stress, volume confirmation, and event/news pressure align.

Required behaviour to encode:

1. Price is at or near long-running resistance on higher timeframes.
2. Resistance is supported by multiple systems, such as VP value area, TLV2 lines, pattern/rectangle boundaries, or repeated highs.
3. Orderbook walls above price evaporate or lose persistence.
4. New liquidity appears lower, or sell-side pressure increases sharply.
5. Wall-zone persistence collapses across nearby zones.
6. News/context attention has risen in the prior days around a plausible market-moving event.
7. Price breaks a lower low.
8. Volume expands and volume pressure agrees with the downside move.

Expected target: larger downside continuation, drawdown risk, or faster move to next support/liquidity/value area.

## Example 2: Quiet Technical Breakout Confluence

Theory: when news is quiet and orderbook is balanced, technical levels may dominate.

Required behaviour to encode:

1. Price is near resistance defined by multiple technical systems.
2. News/context pressure is quiet or normal.
3. Orderbook is balanced, with no extreme wall/pressure warning.
4. Nearby walls/value areas define plausible next targets.
5. Price breaks resistance with volume expansion.
6. Price accepts above the level instead of immediately falling back.

Expected target: continuation toward the next wall, VP level, HVN/LVN boundary, or higher timeframe value area.

## Example 3: Failed Breakout / Exhaustion

Theory: a breakout attempt is vulnerable when price reaches resistance but volume/orderbook/context fail to confirm.

Required behaviour to encode:

1. Price touches or slightly breaks resistance.
2. VP/TLV2/pattern resistance remains active.
3. Volume pressure fades or diverges.
4. Orderbook support fails to rebuild below price.
5. Price returns inside the prior range/value area.

Expected target: failed breakout, reversal, chop, or downside path risk.

## Example 4: Multi-Source Confirmation

Theory: a signal is more valuable when independent systems agree.

Required behaviour to encode:

1. Structure indicates breakout/breakdown/rejection.
2. Orderbook transition agrees with the same direction.
3. Volume confirms the move.
4. Context/news regime explains why technical behaviour may matter or why event risk dominates.
5. Multiple timeframes agree or define a clear path to the next level.

Expected target: stronger and more stable predictive lift than any single source alone.

## Example 5: Downside Momentum After First Break

Theory: sharp downside moves may be easier to assess after the first visible break has started, because panic, forced selling, orderbook movement, and liquidity retreat can build momentum over the next few hours.

Required behaviour to encode:

1. Price has just made an initial downside break, such as a lower low, support break, value-area loss, or fast 1h/3h drop.
2. The prior few hours show whether the market was vulnerable before the break:
   - near support or range low
   - weakening support
   - falling volume quality
   - orderbook support losing persistence
3. The first few hours after the break show whether panic/momentum is building or fading:
   - sell pressure expands
   - buy walls disappear or move lower
   - sell walls chase price downward
   - spread/fragility increases
   - volume remains elevated instead of fading
   - price fails to reclaim the broken support
4. The test should compare:
   - early-break rows where downside pressure keeps building
   - early-break rows where price slows, support rebuilds, or the book stabilizes
5. The goal is risk detection and continuation assessment, not predicting the first tick of the crash from nothing.

Expected target: further downside over the next 3h, 6h, or 24h; max drawdown before bounce; failure to reclaim broken support; or "crash is slowing" if momentum fades.

## Example 6: Signal Stacking And Position Management

Theory: a trade should be adjusted as new evidence arrives, rather than treated as one static entry.

Required behaviour to encode:

1. Same-direction signals from independent sources or timeframes may increase conviction, support add-ons, or justify holding.
2. Opposite-direction signals may reduce exposure, tighten invalidation, or justify partial/full exit.
3. Conflicting signals should be measured as net bullish, bearish, and risk weight across sources.

Expected target: better add/reduce/hold decisions, lower drawdown, or better risk-adjusted return than flat one-entry tests.
